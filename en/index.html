Everything You Need to Know About Studying in Germany

Updated for 2026 German Federal Foreign Office and university regulations.

For university, Studienkolleg, and language course applicants, the mandatory blocked account amount is €11,904 per year (€992 per month), held in your own name at Fintiba, Expatrio, or Coracle and paid out monthly once you arrive in Germany. However, dual Ausbildung applicants whose training contract pays roughly €950–€1,000+ gross per month do NOT need a blocked account.

German public universities have no statutory age limit. For the embassy visa officer, what matters is a coherent, documented justification of any gap years (via employment, skill certifications, and a tailored Motivationsschreiben). For Ausbildung, applicants up to age 35 enjoy very high approval rates.

Yes! Over 1,800 Master’s programs and numerous Bachelor’s programs at German public universities are taught entirely in English. You can gain direct, tuition-free admission with IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL iBT.

Under Germany’s updated Skilled Immigration Act, international students (including Studienkolleg and preparatory language students) can work up to 20 hours per week during the semester (or 140 full days / 280 half days per year) and full-time during semester breaks, earning €13–€16/hour.

Applicants holding a 12-year high school diploma (such as from Iran or Azerbaijan without university credits) complete a 1-year public Studienkolleg in Germany and pass the FSP exam to unlock direct admission to any German public university.

Yes. Master’s, PhD, and Ausbildung visa holders can apply for family reunification (Ehegattennachzug) provided sufficient living space and financial coverage are demonstrated. Spouses receive full, unrestricted work rights in Germany.
